Hopping Across Australia

How kangaroos and wallabies differ across Australia's states, change over time, and align with rainfall patterns.

01 — Where Do They Live?

Kangaroos and wallabies are found across Australia, but different species show very different geographic patterns.

Where are kangaroos and wallabies recorded?

Atlas of Living Australia observation records, 2021–2025. Circle size represents the number of observations in each 0.25° grid cell.

Key takeaway

Observation records are most concentrated in eastern and south-eastern Australia. Western Grey Kangaroo records are more visible in the south-west, while Red-necked Wallaby stands out in Tasmania.

Note: Atlas of Living Australia observation records show where animals were reported. They should not be interpreted as population estimates.

Six species, six different distributions

Viewing each species separately reveals very different geographic patterns across Australia.

Circle size represents the number of Atlas of Living Australia observation records within each 0.25° grid cell.

Eastern concentration

Eastern Grey Kangaroo and Swamp Wallaby records cluster strongly along eastern and south-eastern Australia.

Western pattern

Western Grey Kangaroo records are concentrated mainly across southern and western Australia.

Tasmania stands out

Red-necked Wallaby records show a clear concentration in Tasmania and parts of eastern Australia.

What stands out?

The six species show clearly different recording patterns across Australia. Observation records are especially dense in coastal eastern Australia, while several kangaroo species extend much further into inland and western regions.

Note: Differences in observation density may also reflect where people record wildlife. These records should not be treated as direct measures of animal abundance.

02 — Different States, Different Species

Kangaroo and wallaby records are not distributed evenly across Australia. The mix of species differs substantially between states and territories.

Which species stand out in each state?

Share of kangaroo and wallaby observation records by species within each state and territory, 2021–2025.

East coast

Eastern Grey Kangaroo and wallaby species account for a large share of records in several eastern states.

Western difference

Western Grey Kangaroo contributes a much larger share of records in Western Australia and South Australia.

Tasmania

Tasmania has a distinctive species mix, with Red-necked Wallaby particularly prominent in the observation records.

Percentages describe the composition of observation records within each state. They do not represent the true proportion of animals in the wild.

03 — Rainfall and Wildlife Records

Rainfall varies greatly across Australia and from year to year. Comparing rainfall with wildlife records helps explore whether wetter and drier years align with changes in kangaroo and wallaby observations.

Do wetter years align with more wildlife records?

Annual rainfall compared with Atlas of Living Australia observation records across five Australian regions, 2021–2024.

No single pattern

The relationship between annual rainfall and observation records differs between regions rather than following one consistent national pattern.

Regional differences

Queensland and Western Australia recorded increasing wildlife observations across the period even though rainfall fluctuated between years.

Association, not causation

Rainfall may influence habitat conditions, but wildlife records are also affected by reporting effort and where people make observations.

NSW and ACT are combined because the Bureau of Meteorology reports this rainfall series as NSW/ACT. Only 2021–2024 are compared because the rainfall dataset ends in 2024. Observation records are not direct estimates of animal abundance.
